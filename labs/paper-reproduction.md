# ML Paper Reproduction and Extension

## Goal

Learn how real ML research is performed by independently understanding, reproducing and extending a published machine-learning paper.

The ultimate objective is to progress from consuming research to being able to contribute research ideas.

## Stage 1 — Select a paper

Help me choose a paper with:

- meaningful mathematical or algorithmic content
- manageable compute requirements
- reproducible datasets
- a result that can be tested at small scale
- relevance to modern ML
- room for at least one interesting extension

Do not select a paper simply because code already exists.

Prefer something where I can realistically run individual experiments in roughly 30–60 minutes at reduced scale.

## Stage 2 — Understand before coding

Read the paper section by section.

For each important part, require me to explain:

- the problem being solved
- the baseline approach
- the new idea
- the mathematical formulation
- why the authors expect it to work
- what evidence supports the claim

Reconstruct important equations myself.

## Stage 3 — Reproduction

Implement the essential method.

Establish:

- baseline
- evaluation metric
- controlled experimental setup
- reproduced result

Do not use the authors' implementation as a substitute for understanding.

## Stage 4 — Analyze

Identify:

- assumptions
- weak evidence
- unexplored hyperparameters
- missing ablations
- possible alternative explanations
- failure cases

## Stage 5 — Form my own hypothesis

I should propose at least one modification or new experiment.

Do not immediately tell me what experiment to perform.

Critique my hypothesis as a research mentor would.

## Stage 6 — Experiment

Run controlled experiments comparing:

- original method
- baseline
- my modification
- relevant ablations

Record unsuccessful experiments rather than hiding them.

## Stage 7 — Research-style writeup

Produce a report structured similarly to a research paper:

- abstract
- background
- method
- experimental setup
- results
- ablations
- discussion
- limitations
- conclusion

Clearly separate reproduced findings from my own findings.

## Mentoring philosophy

Gradually reduce guidance throughout the project.

Early on, teach me how to approach research.

By the final stage, behave more like a research supervisor: challenge assumptions, ask questions and critique decisions without deciding everything for me.

Success means that by the end I am making most experimental decisions myself.

## Long-term repetition

Treat this as a repeatable research loop:

1. Paper Reproduction I — heavily guided
2. Paper Reproduction II — moderately guided
3. Paper Reproduction III — mostly independent
